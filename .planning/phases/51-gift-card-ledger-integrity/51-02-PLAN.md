<objective>
Rewrite `redeemGiftCard` and `reloadGiftCard` to claim before they mutate, and to read the ledger —
not `last_tx_ref` — as the idempotency guard.

Purpose: today both functions change a customer balance across four independently-committing
`setValue()` calls with the idempotency key written LAST. A single interrupted execution — no
concurrency needed — leaves the balance already changed while `last_tx_ref` is stale. Redeem
double-debits a customer; reload double-credits (audit H6/H7).

The correction is not reordering (D-04: that just converts a double-debit into a giveaway) and not
one batched `setValues()` (D-05: the mutated columns are not contiguous and it would clobber
`zoho_invoice_number`, which `updateGiftCardInvoice` writes without the lock). It is a durable claim
written in the same failure domain as the balance, before the balance moves.

And the guard cannot be keyed on `tx_ref` alone. Per D-12, `transaction_ref` identifies a payment
ATTEMPT, not a sale: `js/kiosk-core.js:2486-2493` clears `_kioskPaymentKey` on every terminal
outcome including every error branch, and the Retry button re-enters `kioskStartCheckout()` which
mints a fresh `'KIOSK-' + Date.now()` (`:2618-2623`). A crash-then-retry therefore arrives with a
DIFFERENT ref and sails past any ref-keyed guard — which is exactly the hole `last_tx_ref` has
today. Hence: an unsettled claim on the certificate fails closed regardless of ref.

Nothing here is verifiable locally beyond source order — `SpreadsheetApp` does not exist outside
Google. That is stated plainly rather than papered over; 51-03 carries the live probe.

Output: two rewritten money-path handlers and the source-order assertions that guard their shape.
</objective>
